## Wins this week, with proof

Eight fixes shipped and verified against production after deploy. Full evidence
is in `BACKLOG.md`; the four that matter most:

**1. Closed an unbounded duplicate URL space.** `/top-100?page=9`,
`?page=999`, `/latest?page=99` and `?page=5000` all returned HTTP 200 serving
the last real page's content — each under its *own* self-referencing canonical.
That is an unlimited supply of indexable duplicates, and it was created by the
baseline's own pagination fix: `generateMetadata` clamped nothing while the page
body clamped to the last valid page. Out-of-range URLs now answer
`noindex, follow` and canonicalize to page 1, while pages 2-4 keep their own
canonicals. Verified live, all 7 cases.

**2. `/latest` was linking exclusively through redirects.** Every one of its 60
story links pointed at `/story/<clusterId>`, which exists only as a 307 alias to
the slug — 12 of 12 sampled were redirects. The canonical URL never received the
link, and each crawl of the feed cost 60 extra round-trips. Fixed by attaching
the cluster slug at query time. Verified live: 15 of 15 now resolve 200 directly.

**3. SERP snippets were broken copy.** 16 of 30 sampled story descriptions (53%)
ended in a mid-sentence ellipsis. The new `metaDescription()` packs whole
sentences and only truncates when the first sentence alone will not fit. Live
after deploy: 7 of 30 (23%). The residue is not a formatter problem — those are
summaries whose first sentence is itself over-long, which is backlog item 3.
Separately, 29 of 30 titles ran past 60 characters while `" | CurrentWire"` ate
14 of them; long headlines now drop the suffix instead of the headline (25 of 30
live).

**4. The indexable hubs were invisible.** Topic and source hubs become
indexable once they clear the thin-collection bar, but no sitemap listed them.
They are now included — gated by that same bar, so the sitemap can never
advertise a page that answers `noindex`. Live: 258 sitemap URLs including 21
topic and 12 source hubs, 8 sampled all 200 and indexable.

Also shipped: ItemList JSON-LD on /latest, /sources and /source/*; per-page
ItemList URLs on /top-100; hero `fetchPriority` and eager ranked thumbnails
(Next 16 deprecated `Image priority`, and the preload it emitted never reached
the `<img>`); a refreshed llms.txt.

## Losses and unresolved, with cause

**The daily health check failed silently for two days.** 2026-08-17 and
2026-08-18 both failed on GitHub Actions before the daily loop caught and fixed
the cause (news-sitemap emitting out-of-window publication dates). The alarm
fired correctly; nothing was listening. `gh` cannot read this repo's Actions
logs (HTTP 403, needs admin rights), so the loops reproduce the check locally
against production instead. That works, but it means failures are only noticed
when an agent loop runs.

**Story pages are crawl dead ends — the biggest finding of this run.** Measured
across 40 live and archived story pages: 39 have **zero** outbound links to any
other story, and 10 have no topic links either. The "Related coverage" rail does
not render at all; the heading is absent from every page sampled. The cause is
not a defect — round-8 deliberately raised the relatedness bar to two shared
specific entities, and almost no pair of stories clears it. The precision is
right; the consequence is that 1,720 permanent URLs pass no link equity to each
other. The fix (backlog item 1) keeps that bar and adds a separately labelled
navigational rail that makes no relatedness claim.

**Two live category misclassifications** (a Lakers ownership story under
politics, an archaeology story under technology). Filed for `lib/news/` with
benchmark coverage, not patched in templates.

**No CDN caching, still.** Every HTML response is `no-store` and every fetch is
a Vercel MISS. This is the single largest performance lever and it remains open
because it needs careful verification against `unstable_cache`, archived
stories, and both redirect kinds.

## Competitor content-gap snapshot

Structure taken from live robots.txt and sitemaps on 2026-08-18, not from
marketing copy.

**Google News** exposes exactly three indexable hub families in robots.txt:
`/topics/`, `/publications/`, `/stories/`. Publisher hubs are first-class
entities. It blocks every AI crawler by name (GPTBot, ChatGPT-User,
PerplexityBot, anthropic-ai, ClaudeBot, Claude-Web, CCBot).

**AllSides** publishes 7,005 sitemap URLs. The composition is the finding:

| Section | URLs | Share |
|---|---|---|
| `/news-source/*` | 3,696 | 53% |
| `/blog/*` | 2,437 | 35% |
| `/featured/*` | 697 | 10% |
| `/media-bias/*` | 9 | reference pages |

**Ground News** allows GPTBot explicitly, blocks AhrefsBot, and returned 403 to
sitemap requests. Its public identity is per-story coverage distribution — how
many outlets, and how they lean.

**CurrentWire today**: 1,720 story URLs, 21 indexable topic hubs, 12 source
hubs, 9 category pages, 9 trust pages, 0 evergreen articles.

Three concrete gaps follow, all now in the backlog:

1. **Evergreen reference content (backlog 4).** 35% of AllSides' URL inventory
   is non-perishable `/blog/*`, plus 9 `/media-bias/*` pages that carry most of
   their link equity. CurrentWire has none. This is the standard aggregator
   answer to the thin-content ceiling and it can be written honestly from data
   the site already computes.
2. **Coverage breadth has no page (backlog 6).** Ground News's entire identity
   is the signal CurrentWire already computes per story — and CurrentWire hides
   it behind `?sort=most-covered`, which canonicalizes away to `/top-100`.
3. **Publisher hubs are shallow (backlog 8 and 9).** AllSides runs 3,696
   publisher pages; CurrentWire's 12 are limited by feed count, but they also
   carry no per-publisher facts (cadence, category mix, tier rationale) that
   would make them worth ranking.

One competitive advantage worth keeping: CurrentWire's robots.txt allows every
AI crawler, where Google News blocks all of them. For a site whose pitch is
"citable, permanent, attributed", that is the right posture.
