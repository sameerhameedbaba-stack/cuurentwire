## 2. Shipped — `9dc666f`: the retired slug is re-rendered, so redirects cannot pair into a loop

**The defect** (BACKLOG item 1, found 2026-08-26 on a live news-sitemap
URL): a cluster's canonical slug is rebuilt whenever `pickLead()` re-selects
the lead. The retired slug then 307s to the new one — and that redirect is an
ISR entry of its own under the story route's 30-day TTL, which **nothing
invalidates**. The cron revalidates `/story/<canonical slug>`, never the
retired alias, and the redirect is returned from the live branch of
`resolveStoryRequest` before any tagged read, so `revalidateTag` misses it
too. A redirect can outlive its target by weeks; when the headline flaps
back, the fresh redirect points at the frozen one and the pair is permanent.

**Measured first, because the fix is only worth it if renames are common:**

- 5 of 178 archived live stories were slugged differently in the live dataset
  than in the archive at one instant;
- **8 of ~690 news-sitemap stories were renamed in the 32 minutes** spanning
  the 22:00 UTC burst (~15/hour), e.g.
  `at-least-63-americans-missing-in-nepal…` -> `at-least-66-americans-…`.

Headline flapping is the ordinary case, so the loop is a matter of when.

**The fix.** The burst already reads the archive per cluster id, so the
rename costs nothing extra to detect. `readArchivedClusterSlugs()` returns
the stored slug per id and `findNewClusterIds()` is derived from it — one
query, both answers, and it still treats "the archive did not answer" as
"nothing is new" rather than "everything is new" (that distinction now has
its own test, because reading it the other way would announce the whole
dataset to IndexNow on any database hiccup). `archivePublicDataset` records
the slug each upsert retires; the cron burst calls
`revalidatePath('/story/<retired slug>')` for each and reports
`retiredSlugsRevalidated`.

**Live verification** — every one of the 8 slugs retired by the 22:00 burst,
probed immediately after:

```
307 age=0   MISS         -> current canonical OK   (x6)
307 age=0   REVALIDATED  -> current canonical OK
307 age=752 STALE        -> current canonical OK
```

Not one frozen `HIT` pointing at an outdated slug, and every target is the
current canonical. Said honestly: `MISS` alone cannot distinguish "purged by
the burst" from "never cached", so the `REVALIDATED` row is the one that
proves an existing entry was marked. The global check is
`scripts/seo-health.mjs`, which chases every news-sitemap URL hop by hop and
would name any `REDIRECT LOOP`.

Cost: single digits per burst against the 150 canonical revalidations the
same burst already performs, and only when a headline actually changes.

warnings), `npx vitest run` **934 passed / 3 skipped** (8 new), `npm run

## 3. Shipped — `015e2b9`: the ambiguity guard stopped publishing the categories it rejected

**This is BACKLOG item 6's `all`-rail thread, which the 2026-08-26 run flagged
as "the most promising thread here" and left open. It was under-scoped: the
live leak is much larger than that run's sample showed.**

**The mechanism.** `classifyCategory` has an ambiguity guard: on an exact
top-score tie it refuses to choose and files the story in the internal
`general` bucket, which is deliberately `noindex`. But it also pushed the
tied candidates into `CategoryResult.all` — and `all` is copied to
`Article.categories`, which is what `getCategoryData` filters on to build the
"Related coverage" rail on every indexable `/[category]` page
(`lib/news/queries.ts:592`). So the guard protected `articleSection` while
leaking the very categories it had just rejected onto the indexable pages it
exists to protect.

**Measured on production BEFORE the fix.** Nine category pages fetched, their
Related-coverage rails read, then every listed story fetched for its real
`NewsArticle.articleSection`:

| page | general-bucket stories in its rail |
|---|---|
| `/health` | **6 of 6** |
| `/climate` | 4 |
| `/culture` | 3 |
| `/sports` | 3 |
| `/business`, `/technology`, `/politics`, `/science` | 2 each |
| `/world` | 1 |

**All 9 pages leaking, 24 placements across 54 rail slots.** `/health`'s
entire rail was general-bucket stories, including "Talking Shop with Kelly
Rowland". The double-placement mechanism is directly visible: one Jemima
Kirke fashion interview sat on `/culture` AND `/politics`; one emission study
on `/science` AND `/world`; one Maui wildfire story on `/health` AND
`/climate`.

**Measured against the 313-story validated truth set:** the tie path produced
**40 category-page placements and 31 of them (77.5%) did not match the human
label**. That is arithmetic rather than bad luck — a two-way tie lists the
story on both pages and at most one can be correct.

**The fix, at the source as the playbook requires** (classifier defects are
engineered in `lib/news/` with benchmark coverage, never patched in
templates). The ambiguity guard now returns `all: ["general"]` on every path;
the tied ids move to a new `rejectedCandidates` field that is
diagnostics-only and routes nothing. `getCategoryData` additionally refuses
any general-bucket cluster in the rail — not redundancy for its own sake:
~10,000 archived articles still carry `categoriesAll` computed under the old
rule, and the invariant should not depend on data vintage.

**Accuracy is untouched, because `primary` is untouched.** Re-run this run
over `data/local/real-stories.json` + `truth.tsv`: **73.2% exact (229/313),
5.4% wrong-specific (17/313), 110 general (35.1%)** — reproducing the
recorded benchmark figures exactly. Leaked placements **40 -> 0**. Ties are
still detected (20 of them) and still visible for diagnostics; they just no
longer route.

## 4. What is explicitly NOT fixed

Item 6 **stays OPEN**. This closes the leak in one direction — rejected
categories reaching indexable pages. The other half is untouched: **67 of 313
(21%) are stories a human gave a specific category and the classifier routes
to the noindex bucket.** The 2026-08-26 run measured and *refused* the cheap
fixes for that (relaxing the guard roughly doubles wrong-specific errors,
5.4% -> 9.3-11.2%; keyword patching overfits a 313-story snapshot). That half
needs better signals — the shadow `local-minilm` hybrid at 78.3% exact is the
standing candidate — not a threshold nudge. **Do not reopen it as a
regression without comparing against the designed 35% general rate.**

One honest consequence of today's fix: 7 of 9 sampled general-bucket stories
already appeared on no indexable category page, and now it is 9 of 9. That is
the guard's precision-over-recall contract working as designed, and it is the
right trade at a 77.5% error rate. The story pages themselves stay
`index, follow` and stay in both sitemaps — the loss is a listing slot, not a
URL.
