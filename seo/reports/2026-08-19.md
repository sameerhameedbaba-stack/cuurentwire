## 4. Found while working: no robots meta on `pageMetadata()` pages

Not on any backlog, and the largest single find of the run.

`/us`, `/topics`, `/top-100`, `/politics`, `/sources`, `/methodology` and
`/about` carried **no `<meta name="robots">` at all** — verified live on all
seven. Page-level metadata replaces the root layout's wholesale in this Next
version, and `robots: undefined` counts as replacing it with nothing. So every
helper-built page was silently losing `max-image-preview:large` and
`max-snippet:-1`: large-thumbnail eligibility in Discover and Top Stories.

The helper already documented this exact hazard for `openGraph`, one field
above the bug. Verified live after deploy: all seven now carry the full
directive. Guard: `tests/unit/page-metadata-robots.test.ts`.

## 6. What the adversarial reviews caught

This is the part worth keeping. Each of these was in a design that read well
and would have shipped.

- **A fabricated accuracy metric.** The evergreen pages were about to publish
  "on 491 pairs, precision 0.989, recall 0.806". Those come from
  `tests/fixtures/cluster-pairs.ts`, whose own header says every pair is
  invented — fictional towns, companies and people. PLAYBOOK.md's rule is
  "Real-headline accuracy is the only accuracy we quote." The count was stale
  too (501 pairs now). The page publishes **no measured accuracy**; it states
  the CI gates, which are contract values in the repo rather than measurements
  of the world, and says plainly where the pairs come from.
- **Two false prose claims** on the same pages: that an all-press-release
  story's source-mix line prints "0 independent domains" (suppressed by a
  `> 0` guard — the guard was fixed so the sentence became true), and that a
  story's address never changes as coverage grows (`pickLead()` re-selects the
  lead and the slug is built from its title, which is exactly why `/story/`
  307s old addresses).
- **Nine new confident-wrong classifications.** The first archaeology
  dictionary sent "Ancient city of Aleppo faces new shelling" to science —
  "ancient city" is the standard dateline of siege reporting. "Excavation" is
  a construction word as often as an archaeology one; bare "dinosaur" caught a
  film franchise. It also moved **zero** stories on the 313-story real
  production benchmark, so it was buying nothing while adding collisions.
  Narrowed to precise phrases, five entries removed with the breaking headline
  recorded beside each, five collision guards added as fixtures.
- **A cache header that could have lied.** Item 2b originally set a public
  `s-maxage=300` on `/top-100` and `/latest`. On Vercel that header is applied
  by the edge proxy, and no route on this deployment has ever produced a cache
  HIT from a Cache-Control string alone — every measured HIT carries
  `X-Nextjs-Prerender: 1`. If the edge ignored it, the site would have been
  telling every downstream shared cache to hold the two freshness-critical
  pages for five minutes while the origin re-rendered each time, and it would
  have overwritten the `no-store` Next sets on error renders. Split: the TTL
  rides in `Vercel-CDN-Cache-Control` (consumed and stripped at the edge) and
  the client header stays byte-identical to what `/` already serves.

Two more corrections came from measurement rather than review:

- **Item 3 was scoped down.** Its design honestly measured its own output:
  +254 words, of which ~36 were per-story unique and ~218 were a site-wide
  fixed disclosure. Adding 218 words of identical boilerplate to 2,199
  permanent story URLs is mass-duplicated text on a site already fighting a
  duplicate-content ceiling. Shipped the unique parts; the fixed prose lives
  on `/methodology/*` and is linked.
- **Item 10's fix was a deletion.** `next/font` already injects its own
  preloads; the hand-rolled block added duplicates.

## 8. What regressed, and one honest failure

- **The preconnect did not fix the homepage LCP.** It was shipped on the
  hypothesis that third-party connection setup was the constraint. FCP did
  improve (1,912 → 1,492 ms), but LCP moved the wrong way, and measurement
  explains why: the hero image changed from 71 KB to 546 KB between runs.
  Connection setup was never going to beat half a megabyte on a throttled
  link. The first version also emitted two preconnects to the same origin, one
  with `crossorigin` and one without — the CORS-mode connection is never used
  by a no-cors `<img>` fetch, so it cost an extra handshake. Corrected.
- **A health-check flake of my own making.** The `story canonical` check
  compared the canonical against the *sitemap* URL while `get()` follows
  redirects. A story's slug is built from its lead article's headline and
  `pickLead()` re-selects the lead on every refresh, so a slug legitimately
  changing between the sitemap fetch and the story fetch reddened the build
  once. It now compares against the URL actually landed on. A gate that cries
  wolf is a gate people learn to ignore.
- Nothing else regressed. One Playwright failure at `--workers=3` did not
  reproduce at `--workers=2` or in isolation — the concurrency flake
  `MEMORY/2026-08-18-daily-and-weekly-loops-collide.md` documents.

## 9. Open

**Publisher image weight is unbounded and owns the homepage LCP.** Measured
across 12 ranked-list images: median 64 KB, max **2,044 KB**, with CBS at
534–2,044 KB against BBC/Hill/Global at 53–70 KB. `image-upgrade.ts` strips
CBS's signed thumbnail segment to serve the original because the signature
binds exactly one rendition and every other size 404s; `images.unoptimized` is
set because the Vercel optimizer's free tier is ~5K transformations/month and
its wildcard patterns made `/_next/image` an open proxy. Both decisions are
individually right and together they leave no resizing path. This needs a real
decision, not a quick patch — it is written up in `BACKLOG.md` with the
options. Left open deliberately.
