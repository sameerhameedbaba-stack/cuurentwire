## 4. Shipped: durable per-publisher facts on every source hub

**BACKLOG item 3, open for two weeks and described in two weekly reports as
"the largest content gap on the board".** Commit `7457912`, pushed to `main`,
deployed, verified live.

**The problem, measured before the change:** `/source/bbc-news` was 870 words
of which essentially all were the headline text of its own story links, titled
"BBC News — Latest stories", carrying `BreadcrumbList` + a bare `ItemList` and
no publisher-level fact at all. A page in that shape competes only for the
publisher's own brand name, a query the publisher always wins. AllSides and
Ground News hold the durable "&lt;outlet&gt; …" queries with real per-outlet
reference pages.

**What shipped:** `lib/news/source-profile.ts` computes, from the dataset
already in memory (no new IO, no added ISR cost — the same contract as
`hubStats`), the one thing this site can say that nobody else computes: how a
publisher's reporting sits inside a corroborated cluster set. Reports and
distinct stories in the window, how many are also carried by another
publication, how many this site leads with, the widest corroborated story, the
most frequent co-reporting publisher, and the sections actually filed in.

**Live evidence, fetched after the deploy landed:**

```
/source/bbc-news
title   BBC News — coverage and corroboration | CurrentWire   (was "… — Latest stories")
desc    BBC News coverage: 33 stories in the current window, 5 also carried by
        other publications, each linked to the original reporting.
words   870 -> 1040
schema  CollectionPage
        about     {"@type":"Organization","name":"BBC News","url":"https://bbc.com"}
        publisher CurrentWire
        numberOfItems 30 = 30 emitted
robots  index, follow, max-image-preview:large
```

The rendered block on that page:

> In the current snapshot CurrentWire is tracking 33 reports from BBC News
> across 33 stories published over the past 33 hours. 5 of them are also
> carried by at least one other publication CurrentWire ingests, and BBC News
> is the account this site leads with on 33. The most widely corroborated is
> … carried by 5 publications. Across the corroborated stories, the
> publication reporting the same stories most often is The Guardian (2
> shared). Sections filed in: General (11), World (7), Sports (4), Business
> (3), Health (3), Politics (3).

**Honesty properties, enforced in code and in tests, not just in prose:**

- Every count is scoped to the current window and stated as a lower bound over
  the 69 publications this site tracks by name — the page says so.
- A story is counted once however many times one publisher filed into it; an
  unclustered row never counts as a story.
- The widest-corroborated line is absent below two publications, so the copy
  cannot imply breadth that is not there.
- **No bias or factuality rating is rendered or emitted.** That editorial
  position was already the site's; it is now written on the page, the
  authority tier links to `/methodology/publisher-tiers`, and
  `collectionPageSchema` refuses any rating property on the `about` node.
- `about` names the publisher, `publisher` stays CurrentWire. Markup naming
  the outlet as this page's publisher would misrepresent who wrote it; an e2e
  test asserts both directions.

| `/source/cbc-news` | **General (17)**, Politics (10), Business (4), Culture (1), Health (1), Science (1) |
| `/source/the-guardian` | **General (11)**, World (9), Politics (7), Climate (6), Business (4), Sports (3) |
| `/source/npr` | Business (5), Politics (5), Climate (4), Culture (4), General (4), Health (3) |

Independent corroboration: 22 story pages sampled at even intervals through
`/news-sitemap.xml` and read from their own `NewsArticle.articleSection` —
**3 of 22 (14%) are `General`**. `/general` lists 27 stories. One sampled
story is plainly misfiled:
`/story/settler-attacks-threaten-the-west-banks-final-christian-village-cd6dbaa9e7839`
serves `articleSection: "General"` and is a World story by any reading.

**Why this is an SEO problem, not untidiness.** `/general` is `noindex,
nofollow` on purpose — it is the internal low-confidence bucket. A story
routed there therefore appears on **no indexable category page at all**: it
loses the category listing, the category's internal-link path, and the topical
grouping `articleSection` exists to give it. The playbook already ranks wrong
categories as a data-quality defect because they poison category relevance;
this is the same defect one step worse, because the destination is
deliberately invisible.

**Deliberately not fixed today.** The playbook is explicit that classifier
defects are engineered upstream with benchmark coverage, and that the SEO loop
monitors and files them rather than re-fixing the classifier ad hoc. One day's
fetch also cannot say whether 14% is the designed low-confidence rate or a
regression. The source hubs now publish that share continuously, so the next
run can measure the trend with no new tooling.

## 7. What regressed

Nothing on the live site. One thing about the *test harness* got worse and is
now understood.

**The Playwright suite is flaky at its default worker count, and it is load,
not a site bug.** The 2026-08-24 report logged "a one-off 500 on `/`" with the
note *"if it recurs, it is real"*. It recurred. Across **five full runs of the
same unchanged tree** today, three failed and **each failed a different
test** — `seo.spec.ts` "the sitemap lists the reference pages",
`briefing.spec.ts` "/briefing serves today's briefing", `top-10.spec.ts` "the
internal general bucket has no top-10 page". Every one of them passed when its
own spec ran alone (38/38, 10/10). The full suite was green twice at 111
passed, and green at `--workers=2`. A failure that moves to a different test
every run and disappears under less concurrency is the harness saturating.

Honest gap: **the failure detail was never captured.** The artifacts were
wiped by the passing re-runs before they could be read, so whether these are
500s or timeouts is still unknown. That is why pinning `workers` in
`playwright.config.ts` is *not* ranked as work — a config change that hides an
intermittent 500 would be the worst possible outcome. Capture the reason
first.

**Also unproven rather than regressed:** the `url-survival` LOST fix. The
workflow's last two runs are `failure` (08-23 07:03, 08-24 07:30 UTC) and both
predate `c124d70`, which shipped later on 08-24. The fix itself was verified
against production (`GONE=0 UNAVAILABLE=0 LOST=214`, exit 0), so the code is
known good; the workflow has simply not run since. The 2026-08-25 run is the
first that exercises it, and `[auto-alert]` #1 should not be closed before it
goes green.
