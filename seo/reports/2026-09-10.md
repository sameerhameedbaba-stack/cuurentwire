**Nothing was down.** In the same hour `seo-health.mjs` passed every check, all

thirteen minutes old. A healthy site was serving four permanent 5xx.

### Why the 500 was permanent

`lib/news/story-resolution.ts` deliberately answers a retriable 500, not a 404,
when the archive says "no such story" for a slug carrying a real cluster-id
token. That is correct: writes are batched to ~25–30 minutes
(`lib/database/persist-gate.ts`), so a story can be live, in the news sitemap,
and genuinely absent from the archive inside that window, and a 404 is the one
answer that cannot be taken back.

**But the shield has no clock in it.** It cannot tell "absent four minutes"
from "absent eleven hours" from "absent forever", so the only thing that ever
ends the 500 is a human writing the id into `data/lost-stories.json`. Memory
note: `seo/MEMORY/2026-09-09-a-window-shield-with-no-expiry-is-permanent.md`.

## 2. What this run did NOT fix, and will not pretend it did

**Why were four stories advertised in a sitemap and never written to the
archive, while the archive was up?** Unknown. The tombstone clears the symptom
and answers nothing.

What is actually known:

- They are the **first never-alive URLs since 2026-08-21**. Of 11,516 ledger
  entries only **7** carry `lastOk: null`: three from the August Neon outage,
  and these four.
- They arrived as **one batch on one day**, not as a daily drip.
- All four are newswire/corporate press releases — mining drill results, an
  ASML/Xanadu collaboration.
- **A same-event cluster merge is not supported for three of the four**: the
  strings `koryx`, `aldebaran` and `cuprita` appear **nowhere** in
  `archive-sitemap.xml`. (Xanadu does appear, but not this ASML story.)

What is **not** known: whether the cluster left the live dataset before a
persist burst, was filtered out, or something else. A mechanism is not being
published as a finding here.

**The cheap next step is instrumentation, not a rewrite.** Nothing today
records when a cluster enters the news sitemap versus when its archive row is
written, so the gap that orphans a story is invisible between two daily probe
runs — and the probe runs once a day, which is why these URLs were broken for
eleven hours before anyone looked.

The shield's missing clock was deliberately **not** changed today. It is
correct for the window it was written for, and moving the tombstone check past
the archive lookup would reintroduce the 500-during-outage case the current
ordering exists to prevent. Fixing one thing by breaking another is the exact
pattern the standing order forbids.
