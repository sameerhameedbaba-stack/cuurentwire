# 3. Shipped this run

## `e4714d1` — one event posted twice under two cluster ids

**Found by reading the live Bluesky feed.** Two posts, 2026-09-05 15:21 UTC and
17:25 UTC, with **byte-identical text**: *"US hits three Iranian oil tankers
after saying its warships were targeted"*. Different cluster ids —
`cfd84c3e6f25d` and `ceee985710f14` — so the cluster-id dedup shipped on 09-03
could not see them.

The archive itself agrees they are one story. `/api/stats/archive-sources`
reports `cfd84c3e6f25d` first seen **14:25:06Z** and `ceee985710f14` first seen
**14:30:18Z** — two clusters opened five minutes apart for one event — and
`cfd84c3e6f25d` is now `merged: true`, 308-ing to the other's canonical slug.
The merge happened *after* both posts went out.

So the 09-03 fix was right and incomplete: keying on the cluster id closed the
headline-rewrite duplicate, but it assumed one story is one cluster. The poster
already holds the signal that catches a split — the headline it is about to
publish. It now keeps a second stateless ledger built from the text of its own
recent posts and skips a candidate whose normalised headline is already there.
A stored post truncated at the 280-character limit matches by prefix; anything
looser would let one long headline swallow unrelated stories.

**The boundary this moves is the definition of "the same story", so it ships
with tests on both sides of it:** the identical tanker headlines must dedupe,
and the two genuinely different Ukraine-envoy headlines of 2026-09-06 — *"US
envoys meet Putin in Moscow for Ukraine talks"* and *"US envoys meet Zelensky in
Ukraine after talks with Putin in Russia"*, both live, both correct — must
**both** post. Six new tests; negative control run before commit, and breaking
the function fails three of them.

**Not verified live, and stated as such.** The proof is the absence of a
duplicate on the next cluster split, which is a future event. The next run
should re-read the feed rather than assume it.

*Left alone deliberately:* the two Ukraine-envoy posts above are arguably one
developing story shared twice in four hours. They carry different headlines
because they describe different events, and collapsing them would need an
editorial judgement about story identity that no cheap signal supports. Skipping
a real development is worse than an occasional near-repeat.
