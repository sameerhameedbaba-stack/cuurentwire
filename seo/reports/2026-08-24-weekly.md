Twenty-four stories on a day that should have had six hundred. Those stories
were live, were advertised in `/news-sitemap.xml`, were never written to
`story_archive`, and then aged out of the ~72 h live dataset. **The content
exists nowhere. No restore brings it back.**

Two things follow, and only one of them was fixable this run.

**Fixed: the gate could not go green.** `url-survival` classified these as
UNAVAILABLE — "the origin is having a bad day, retry" — and failed the
workflow every night since 2026-08-22 for a condition that will never clear.
That is precisely the failure the GONE/UNAVAILABLE split was written to
prevent, arriving from the other direction: a permanently red gate stops being
read, and it buries the next real regression. A third state now exists —
**LOST** (5xx and not seen alive for three days), reported in full every run
and not a build failure. The guard that makes it safe is a 90% healthy-share
threshold: during a real outage almost everything is 5xx, the share collapses,
nothing is reclassified, and the gate goes red exactly as before. A site-wide
outage can never be laundered into "those pages were lost anyway". Verified
against production minutes apart: `UNAVAILABLE=214, exit 1` → `GONE=0
UNAVAILABLE=0 LOST=214, exit 0`.

**Still open: the status code.** Those 214 URLs answer a permanent 500.
Crawlers retry forever and drop them eventually anyway, so the 500 buys
nothing and costs crawl budget. The obvious fix is to 404 them, and it is
**not obviously right** — the guard producing that 500 is the one that fixed
the 2026-08-22 Google News bug, where brand-new stories 404ed out of the news
sitemap because batched writes mean "the archive has not heard of it" no
longer means "it never existed". Getting it wrong in the eager direction
re-creates that bug on the one crawl that decides Google News entry. What is
missing is a time bound, and the slug does not carry one: cluster ids are
content hashes with no timestamp. It needs a design and a review, not a patch
at the end of an audit. Backlog item 1.

Contained, and checked rather than assumed: **0 of the 214 appear in any
sitemap.**

## The CWV probe was measuring page order, not page speed

Last week's report published `/` LCP 7,376 → 4,604 ms swings and a TTFB of
2,842 ms against curl's 134–386 ms warm, declined to call it a regression, and
said the facet had no usable trend line. This run found out why.

Every URL gets a fresh `BrowserContext`, but Chromium shares its socket pool
across contexts, so **only the first navigation of a run pays DNS + TCP +
TLS**. Under the 150 ms emulated latency this run measured `/` at TTFB
**2,844 ms** and `/top-100`, two contexts later, at **131 ms** — and 131 ms is
*below one emulated round trip*, which is only possible on a reused socket.
Whichever page ran first absorbed the entire connection setup. The series was
measuring list order.

One throwaway navigation now warms the connection before anything is measured.
Same session, same site, minutes apart:

| Surface | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| `/` LCP | 5,104 ms | **1,852 ms** |
| `/` TTFB | 2,844 ms | **102 ms** (curl: 135 ms warm) |
| `/` FCP | 3,664 ms | **1,468 ms** |
| `/top-100` LCP | 1,220 ms | 1,344 ms |
| story LCP | 1,532 ms | 1,328 ms |

**This is a measurement fix, not a speed win, and next week must not read it
as one.** History entries now carry a `warmedConnection` flag so the
discontinuity is machine-visible; the drop across that boundary is the
instrument. What it does buy is real: the facet finally has numbers worth
trending, and all three surfaces sit under the 2.5 s LCP threshold that
Discover eligibility depends on.

A second defect in the same script: `firstLiveStoryUrl()` probed with
`redirect: "follow"`, so it accepted a URL that 308s to its canonical slug and
baked an extra round trip into every story-page number in the history. Story
slugs are rebuilt whenever `pickLead()` re-selects the lead, so retired
addresses are the normal case, not an edge one. Now `manual` — the next run
skipped a 308 and a 307 and measured a real canonical URL.

That is four defects found in this one script in two weeks, all of the same
family: **the instrument breaks first and quietly.**

## Competitor content gap

Evidence-based only — what was crawled or searched this run, not a feature
wishlist.

**AllSides.** `robots.txt` 200, `Allow: /`, one flat `sitemap.xml`. It
publishes bias ratings for **1,400+ media outlets and writers** as dedicated
per-outlet reference pages, plus a long tail of dated editorial content going
back to 2012 (`/content/<month>-<year>`, `/blind-survey/<month>-<year>` — both
visible in the sitemap with `lastmod` values as recent as 2025-10). That
archive is evergreen, non-news, and accumulates.

**Ground News.** `robots.txt` 200 with `Allow: /`, and it blocks YandexBot and
AhrefsBot by name. Distinct indexable page types visible on the homepage:
per-story coverage pages, topic/interest pages, geographic region pages,
country feeds, a Blindspot feed, per-source pages, dated daily briefings, and
bias/ownership/factuality rating pages. Its ratings are the *average of three
external raters* (AllSides, Ad Fontes, Media Bias Fact Check) rather than a
proprietary rubric.

**Google News.** `robots.txt` disallows nearly everything and allow-lists
`/topics/`, `/publications/`, `/stories/` and the home shell — its "Full
Coverage" per-story multi-perspective view is largely closed to crawlers, so
it is a demand competitor, not an organic-SERP one.

**The concrete, measurable gap.** CurrentWire has **38** `/source/<slug>` hubs
and they carry no durable per-publisher content at all. Measured this run:
`/source/bbc-news` is 737 words, and essentially all 737 are headline text
from 35 story links; the title is "BBC News — Latest stories"; there is no
prose about the publisher and no `Organization`/`Periodical` schema for the
publisher itself. `/source/espn` is 310 words. Against 1,400+ outlet pages at
AllSides and rated source pages at Ground News, CurrentWire's source hubs
compete only for the publisher's own brand name — a query the publisher always
wins.

The honest framing matters here: CurrentWire deliberately publishes **no**
bias or factuality rating, and its authority tiers are explicitly not one.
That is a standing editorial position and this gap does not argue for changing
it. The gap is that the hubs publish *nothing durable*, while every input
already exists and is already computed — tier and its rationale, cadence,
category mix, first seen, story count, share of multi-source stories. Same
shape as the `/methodology/*` pages, whose figures are computed at render so
they cannot drift from the code. Backlog item 3.

Two things CurrentWire has that neither competitor's crawl showed: coverage
breadth stated as an explicit **lower bound** with its limits on the page
(`/most-covered`), and a published, code-derived merging methodology. Those are
the differentiators worth deepening rather than trading away.
